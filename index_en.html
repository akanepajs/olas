Cage-free share of egg listings at Latvian retailers

Online catalogue snapshot by SKU. Latest data: 2026-11-04 (updated daily).
Latvia's large online grocers list almost only cage-free eggs by stock-keeping unit (SKU) count: Rimi 94%, Barbora (Maxima) 100%, Lidl 100% (n=1), top! 100% (n=1). That is well above the national cage-free production share (about 47% of laying-hen places; Eglitis and Kanepajs 2026). This is a shelf-presence indicator, not a sales-volume share.

Price per egg

Price per egg (pack price divided by the number of eggs per pack, so 10-, 12-, and 15-egg packs compare fairly). The dearest are Rimi's free-range eggs (0.40 EUR/egg); the cheapest are Rimi's barn eggs and Lidl's barn eggs (0.16 EUR/egg). Median by production type (EUR/egg): organic 0.34 (n=1), free-range 0.33 (n=5), barn 0.26 (n=22), caged 0.18 (n=1). Medians by retailer: Rimi 0.30 EUR/egg, Barbora 0.25 EUR/egg, Lidl 0.16 EUR/egg, top! 0.24 EUR/egg.

Method

Every chicken shell-egg listing in each retailer's online catalogue was read and classified by EU production code (0 organic, 1 free-range, 2 barn, 3 caged), using the egg-marking code in the name (Nr.0/1/2/3) and/or production keywords (kuti detas = barn, sprostos = caged, brivas turesanas = free-range, eko/bio = organic). Quail eggs and egg products (egg white, etc.) were excluded. Cage-free % = codes 0/1/2 over all chicken shell-egg listings. Every listing carried a production label (0 unknowns), so the national-anchor adjustment leaves the figure unchanged.

Coverage

Of six large chains, four have a machine-readable online egg catalogue (Rimi, Barbora/Maxima, Lidl, and top!; Lidl carries a small egg range, and etop.lv shows only TOP's own-brand eggs). The others:

  • Mego: Mego (mego.lv) has no online store (informational site only); no egg catalogue.
  • Elvi: Elvi (elvi.lv) eggs page shows only one expired promo item (Balticovo, 18 eggs, 0.17 EUR/egg, undated) with no housing type, so it cannot be classified and is not counted.

Limitations

  • Shelf presence by SKU count, not sales volume. A retailer with 90% cage-free SKUs could still sell many caged eggs if the cheap, high-volume lines are caged.
  • Lidl and top! list only one egg product online, so their cage-free share and median price rest on a single product and may not reflect the whole store shelf (etop.lv shows only TOP's own TIP TOP brand).
  • The 47% reference is production capacity (laying-hen places), not retail volume.
  • Price is the listed price (may include promotions) divided by egg count; not weighted by sales volume.

Sources

  • Scraped online catalogues: Rimi, Barbora, Lidl, top!.
  • Eglitis and Kanepajs (2026), Economic analysis of a laying-hen cage ban in Latvia (national cage-free share).
  • European Commission, egg marketing standards (codes 0/1/2/3).
  • Code and data: https://github.com/akanepajs/olas.
